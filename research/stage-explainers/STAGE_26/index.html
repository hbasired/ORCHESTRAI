3. What the build process CAUGHT (running the real loop finds real defects)

  1. found Open-circuit material loop: SimWorld suppliers fulfilled orders into a counter — deliveries never fed stage buffers, so no policy could prevent stockouts. Fixed IN THE SIM (the honest place): on_fulfil callback + deliver_material().
  2. found Winner-take-all monoculture: the first observed supplier won every award forever; a single failure would have had no measured fallback. Fixed: deterministic exploration rounds (counter-based, no RNG).
  3. found Capacity clamp on the wrong quantity: clamping order qty by capacity − position (instead of physical on-hand) trickled 1-unit orders whenever ROP > capacity — starving the plant while "ordering". The (s,S) trigger stays position-based; the gate is physical.
  4. found FIFO lead attribution smears: attributing completions to the oldest pending order assigned a delayed order's lead to faster later ones — the injected disruption was invisible. Fixed: exact per-order leads measured in the delivery callback.
  5. found The latency detector took FOUR refuted iterations: last-element z-test (missed mid-batch) → per-lead outlier test (refuted by the review's control arm: natural-tail multiple-testing; and structurally LATE for freeze-type delays whose fulfilments only appear after the freeze) → placement-windowed shift test (killed the fulfilment-order ramp artifact: same-placement batches fulfil shortest-first) → overdue-pending (the operational expediting rule — watches unfulfilled orders' AGE, fires during the freeze). Plus a registry-integrity fix (leaked phantom pendings from failure-pruning/exact-match mismatch).

Each finding came from RUNNING the loop against the real sim and refusing to accept an unexplained number — the same discipline that caught the Stage-25 load-test defects.
