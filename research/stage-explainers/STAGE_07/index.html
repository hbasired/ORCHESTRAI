1. What this stage built & the honest verdict

Stage 7 turns the INTERVENE step of the self-healing loop (KB_25 step 4) from a single deterministic rule into a real, trained reinforcement-learning substrate: a from-scratch PPO agent that learns to allocate a contended maintenance crew across machines that crack at overlapping times — wrapped in a hard safety shield and evaluated honestly against the existing rules.

The honest verdict: the rules are near-optimal at this scope, PPO does not beat them, so the rules remain the default chooser. PPO ships trained + safety-shielded as the learnable substrate for Stage 8.

This is the project's "the better policy wins, not the fancier one" rule applied literally. We built a real RL system, tested it rigorously, and reported that it doesn't (yet) earn the default slot — which is far more valuable and credible than forcing a marginal win on a problem that doesn't need RL yet.

4. The safety shield (shielded RL)

The ml-engineer mandate forbids shipping a policy that "allows known-unsafe actions even at low probability." A stochastic neural policy can't satisfy that alone. So the learned net is wrapped by a hard shield: if the crew is free and any at-risk machine's risk ≥ critical (0.85), maintenance is FORCED on the highest-risk machine regardless of what the net sampled. Observed live: the raw net chose stage 9 (wasteful), the shield forced the genuinely at-risk stage 3. Verified by test_shield_forces_maintenance_on_critical_proximity. This guarantees the shipped policy never ignores a near-certain failure — the safety floor is a rule, the learning sits on top. (Still sim-only; the Stage-17 functional-safety wrapper must precede any real-actuator use.)

5. Why rules win — and why that's the right call

  • The problem is near-trivial at v0. One intervention type (preventive maintenance), one crew, uniform crack ETAs → "if a machine is at-risk and the crew is free, maintain the most-urgent one" is almost optimal. The eval shows greedy and priority rules tie, and both leave only 0.375 breakdowns/episode.
  • PPO is not broken — it learns a trivial bandit cleanly and beats no-intervention here 4×. It simply has little headroom to beat a near-optimal rule.
  • Forcing a "win" would be theatre. Given the high-variance downtime metric (the Stage-6 audit's G-046 warning), it would be easy to cherry-pick a seed where PPO looks better. We refused: CRN pairing + CIs + reporting the unfavourable result.
  • The value is the substrate, not a v0 victory: a reusable RL env + auditable PPO + paired-CI eval harness + the shielded-RL safety pattern — the foundation Stage 8 needs when the action space grows beyond what hand-rules can cover.
